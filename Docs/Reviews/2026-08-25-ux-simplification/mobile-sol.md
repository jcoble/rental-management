# Mobile UX simplification — flow and plumbing — SOL medium

The mobile app has capable workflows underneath, but it routinely presents back-office structure before the landlord’s immediate task. The biggest simplification would come from shortening property setup, turning vacancy-to-lease into one guided path, and making expense entry receipt-first.

Counts below are minimum happy-path estimates from Today. Sheets and dialogs count as screens; taps exclude typing, picker interactions, applicant actions, and third-party websites.

## The landlord's day (10 lines): what the app currently makes them do

1. Choose between “Sandbox” and “Live” before adding the first rental.
2. Describe ownership, rental structure, status, fees, and tax basis before the first property exists.
3. Navigate among Properties, Units, Applications, Tenants, and Leases to fill one vacancy.
4. Maintain a listing workspace and separate Zillow publication metadata.
5. Convert an approved application through four move-in preparation steps.
6. Understand agreement versions, fingerprints, issuance, memberships, access grants, and account states.
7. Open five-step work-order forms for issues that are usually “where, what happened, photo.”
8. Open four-step expense forms containing owner billing and vendor tax fields to record a receipt.
9. Reconcile a Today briefing with separate money, message, and work-order sections.
10. End a tenancy through separate ending-plan, possession-return, account, access, turnover, and deposit workflows.

The landlord shell exposes five tabs—Today, Rentals, Money, Work, and Inbox—but the core hubs expand to 21 destinations: six each under Rentals, Money, and Work, plus three under Inbox ([home_shell.dart:85](/home/blackcolours/dev/work/rental-management/mobile/lib/features/home/home_shell.dart:85), [mobile_destination.dart:260](/home/blackcolours/dev/work/rental-management/mobile/lib/features/home/mobile_destination.dart:260)).

### Add property/unit

- Accidental complexity: A landlord adding their own house sees an owner selector, an “add owner” subflow, management fee, purchase/land values, in-service date, and depreciation treatment. - Proposal: Make the initial sheet one page: rental name, address autocomplete, monthly rent, bedrooms, and bathrooms. ### Move-in

- Today: approximately 4 screens / 6 taps / no new required fields in the current possession confirmation / concepts: executed governing agreement, resident household, tenant account, unit availability, and access. ## The three worst offenders

1. **Add property / first-run setup.** It asks for 20 controls and management/accounting structure before the user sees value. The single most helpful change is to create a rental from name, address, rent, beds, and baths, defaulting everything else.

2. **Vacancy-to-signed-lease.** One outcome is fragmented across Units, Applications, lease preparation, agreement management, and issuance. The single most helpful change is a unit-level vacancy timeline whose only prominent control is the next action.

3. **Expense capture.** A frequent phone task starts as a four-step accounting form instead of a camera action. The single most helpful change is receipt-first capture followed by a three-field confirmation.

## Keep — intrinsic complexity that must stay, and why

- Email verification and secure authentication: necessary to protect financial, identity, and lease information.
- Lease dates, rent, deposit, parties, and the exact document being signed: these determine legal and financial obligations.
- Signature progress and immutable signed documents: required evidence, although version IDs and fingerprints need not lead the screen. The API has a dedicated signature-progress endpoint ([LeaseAgreementController.cs:79](/home/blackcolours/dev/work/rental-management/RentalCommand.Api/Controllers/LeaseAgreementController.cs:79)).
- Screening consent, consumer-report source, decision records, and adverse-action handling: legally consequential; reveal them at the relevant decision rather than during ordinary listing work.
- Payment amount, date, method, and final allocation: money must reconcile, but the common allocation can be defaulted.
- Move-out notice dates, possession date, deductions, refund, and statement: legally and financially significant.
- Unit availability and overlapping-lease checks: essential server invariants; show plain corrective messages instead of internal lifecycle terminology.
- Explicit transaction boundaries for move-in, move-out, deposits, account state, and access changes: complexity belongs under the UI, not in separate user-operated commits.
