Cash flow shows how much cash your properties produced during a period after operating costs and full loan payments.

## The five lines

1. **Rent and other operating cash in** is cash received from normal property operations.
2. **Operating expenses** are cash costs such as repairs, utilities, insurance, and management costs for the period.
3. **Net operating income (NOI)** is operating cash in minus operating expenses.
4. **Debt service** is the full cash paid toward property loans, including principal and interest.
5. **Net cash flow** is NOI minus debt service.

In short:

**Operating cash in − operating expenses = NOI**

**NOI − debt service = net cash flow**

## A simple example

If a property brings in $8,000, has $3,000 of operating expenses, and has $2,200 of loan payments:

- NOI is $5,000.
- Debt service is $2,200.
- Net cash flow is $2,800.

That $2,800 is the cash produced before owner contributions or distributions and other activity outside property operations.

## Cash flow is not the same as profit

Loan principal uses cash but is not an expense on the profit and loss statement. Depreciation is an expense on that statement but does not use cash during the period. Because of those differences, profit and cash flow can both be correct and still show different amounts.

## What this view leaves out

Refundable tenant deposits are not operating income. Owner contributions and distributions are also not property operating performance. They can change total cash on hand, but they are kept out of the property cash-flow result so the result stays useful.

Use cash flow to see whether operations and loan payments are producing or consuming cash. Use the Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, and general ledger when you need the broader accounting picture.
