Audit-ready books are records you can explain and support. They do not guarantee that an audit will be easy or that every entry is correct. They do reduce the scramble to rebuild a story from scattered spreadsheets, bank statements, texts, and receipts.

## What strong records show

For an important money event, you should be able to answer:

- What happened?
- When did it take effect, and when was it entered?
- Which property, unit, tenant, vendor, or owner did it concern?
- How did it affect the books?
- What receipt, invoice, lease, bank match, or note supports it?
- Was it later corrected, and why?

Rental Command keeps the source record and its balanced accounting entry connected so those answers do not depend on memory.

## What spreadsheets often get wrong

A spreadsheet can total a column, but it rarely protects the history behind that total. Common problems include:

- overwriting an old amount with the corrected amount;
- deleting a row that no longer looks right;
- mixing deposits held for tenants with rent income;
- counting a bank transfer as new income or a new expense;
- losing the receipt or explanation behind a line;
- using different category names from month to month; and
- letting several copies of the same sheet disagree.

These problems may stay hidden until a tax return, owner question, refinance, sale, or audit requires a clear answer.

## Corrections should preserve the story

Posted records are not quietly rewritten. A credit, reversal, refund, or other correction keeps the original event and adds the dated correction. That shows what changed without pretending the first record never existed.

## Your part still matters

Good software cannot decide whether a cost is allowable, a deduction is legal, or a tax treatment is correct for your situation. Enter transactions promptly, attach supporting documents, reconcile to the bank, review unusual balances, and involve a qualified professional when needed.

The result is not “nothing can go wrong.” The result is a set of books that is easier to inspect, explain, and correct.
